The loop

How it works

Three steps, always in this order. You can't race a car you haven't seen with your own eyes.

1

Spot

Walk, drive, wait at a red light. Anything on four wheels in the real world can be caught: a daily commuter, a weekend roadster, a supercar parked downtown.

2

Scan

Take the photo with the StreetCaught camera and frame the car. An AI identifies the make and model, then the card lands in your garage with its real specs, its rarity and a random finish. When it can't tell two generations apart, you pick the right one: you're the one looking at the car.

3

Race

Pick five cards for five events. You don't drive: the race is simulated from each car's stats, the track and the surface. Your job is choosing the right car for each event.

Rule 1 · Rarity

Rarity comes from the factory

A card's tier is set by how many units of that generation were really built. It never depends on luck or on the player. Common cars are common because the streets are full of them.

From Epic up, the card glows in its tier color. From Legendary up, a brighter spot slowly travels around the frame. The first time you catch a model, its XP is tripled.

Classics get a wooden frame

A car becomes a classic 30 years after its generation stopped being built. The end of production counts, not the start, so even the last car off the line is 30. Nobody picks favourites: it's an age, and every January 1st another year of cars joins the club.

A classic trades its tier border for a thicker wooden frame with real grain. The tier still shows in the text, the glow and the word at the bottom of the card.

Where the real production figure is unknown, the card carries a small EST. badge. Estimated entries never land above Rare: the top tiers are reserved for the 259 cars whose numbers have been checked by hand. A figure that only counts US sales, not worldwide production, can lift a card to Rare at most.

Rule 2 · Finishes

Two things on every card

Rarity belongs to the car. The finish belongs to your copy, rolled fresh on every scan. A holo Corolla is possible, and that's on purpose. The border tells you the tier, the background tells you the finish.

Finish odds per scan

Tier colour

The background wears the tier

Carbon, Forged and Holo cards take the colour of the car's tier. A Legendary holo glows gold, a Mythic forged card has red resin flakes. Gold and Silver keep their brushed metal.

Holo foil

A second roll on top

One card in a hundred that already has a finish also gets a holo foil laid over it: a Gold Holo, a Carbon Holo. It keeps its own background and adds the foil that lights up when the light passes. On a Minimal card it shows as a ★.

Light

Tilt to see it

A holo foil only shows inside the band of light that crosses the card when you tilt your phone. Carbon is a material: you see it even in the shade, and the light adds a shine on top. Holo patterns carry a number from 0 to 999, so no two copies shine the same way.

Full or Minimal

Full shows everything: the car picture running edge to edge with its name printed on it, generation and year, the four stats with bars, the real specs (0-100 km/h, top speed, power, torque, redline, drive, fuel, units built) and the money block (MSRP and market range).

Minimal keeps only what you need to pick a car: the picture with the model name, the drivetrain, the four stats and the Power. That's the view for comparing five cars at a glance. Switch between them in the garage.

Power and its letter

The number in the bottom corner is the car's Power, from 20 to 100. It's what counts against your hand budget. The letter says the same thing at a glance:

Rule 3 · Racing

Real specs in, four stats out

Every card lists the car's real figures: 0–100 km/h, top speed, power, torque, redline, drivetrain. The game turns them into four stats from 1 to 100.

Rule 4 · Campaign

Six countries, one new problem each

Ten levels per country, five races per level. The events and the rival's hand are fixed, so you can study them before you pick your five cards. Each country opens at a player level and adds one condition you haven't had to handle yet.

Stars

Share of the points

Stars come from your share of the points, not the number of events won. Dominating three events beats scraping four. Three stars need 80% of the points.

Rewards

A part at levels 5 and 10

Three stars on level 5 and level 10 of a country earn a part, the first time only. The level 10 part comes with much better odds.

The one legendary

United Kingdom, level 10

The last level of the campaign gives a guaranteed Legendary part. Elsewhere, only an event podium can roll one.

Coming back to an early country with a stronger garage? Your hand is capped at that country's Power, so it stays a real race.

Rule 5 · Events

Two weekly tournaments

The campaign rewards progress. Events reward a wide garage. Every Monday two new challenges open, one easy and one hard, and each one asks for five different cars that all meet the same condition.

Easy

About a third of the catalog fits

Uncommon or better, all-wheel drive, or cars built in the USA, Japan or Germany. A garage of thirty cars usually has five that qualify.

9

A live standings board

Eight rivals sit on the board, from −8 to +8 points. You enter in the middle at zero. Each race is against the rival just above you.

8

Eight races, blind hands

You never see the rival's cars before the start, so you build a hand instead of answering one. Winning earns stars that the rival loses, and losing works the other way, so the board reshuffles after every race.

3

Podium pays once

After the eighth race, your place is final. The top three win a part: 80% at the promised tier, 15% one tier up, 5% two tiers up. A hard-event win can drop a Legendary.

An event race costs one fuel, like a campaign race. Rivals are calibrated on your best legal hand at the start of the week, so a car you catch or a part you fit mid-week shows up as places on the board.

Rule 6 · Friends

Challenge a friend with a code

No server, no account. You set the rules, pick your five cars, and the game turns it all into a short code. Send it by text, Discord or anything else. Your friend pastes it, picks five cars without seeing yours, and the race plays out on their phone.

1

Set the rules

A Power cap, one car rule (any car, a drivetrain, a rarity, one make or one country) and the five events with their weather. Draw them at random or pick each one.

2

Pick your hand

Your cars travel with their upgrades and parts, and with their finish: a holo card arrives as that same holo, same pattern number.

3

Send the code

Your friend pastes it in Friends → Join a game. Both phones compute exactly the same result from the same code, so nobody can argue about who won.

The Power cap replaces both players' own limit, and it can go up to 500, higher than anyone reaches by leveling. A level 5 player and a level 40 player race with exactly the same budget, and it's the only place in the game where the five most expensive cars fit in one hand.

Want your friend to see your actual photos? Tap Send with photos instead: the challenge goes out as a small .streetcaught file that opens straight in the app. Cards that came from a pack show their silhouette.

Rerolls

Three a day

A quest you can't finish, like "Catch 3 BMW" in a neighborhood without any, can be swapped. Watch an ad for three new quests, up to three times a day. New quests start from zero.

Stacking

One car, two quests

A single catch counts for every quest it fits. An AWD Subaru moves "Catch 5 cars" and "Catch 3 AWD cars" at the same time. Makes in quests are ones you see everywhere: Toyota, Honda, Ford, BMW and the like.

Rule 8 · The Workshop

Upgrade, merge, fit parts

Duplicates aren't wasted. Catch the same model again, any generation, and it becomes fuel for the Workshop. Everything is stored per model: if you own three Civics, you sacrifice two to upgrade "the Civic", and that's the one you race.

Coin costs follow the car's Power, so tuning a hypercar costs far more than tuning a city car. A tuned car's Power goes up too: faster in one event, less room in your hand for the other four.

Parts are real hardware

A part never says "better in the rain". It changes something physical (power, mass, top speed, downforce, launch traction, braking, tire grip on each surface) and the race physics decides where that pays off. A GT wing adds downforce, which helps in corners. A drag race has no corners, so there it only costs you drag.

Tiers

Five tiers, one bigger bonus

Parts come in Common ×1, Uncommon ×1.3, Rare ×1.7, Epic ×2.3 and Legendary ×3. The tier multiplies the bonus, never the penalty: a Legendary slick grips better on dry asphalt and slides exactly as much on snow. From Epic up, most parts become different hardware, like a twin turbo instead of a single.

Slots

Six big, two small

Each big slot takes its own family: turbo, transmission, carbon, wheels, suspension, aero. Small slots take brakes, an exhaust or a set of tires. Every car has one small slot from the start, and a second one can be bought for a single model. A part can only sit on one car at a time.

Drops

Won after races

Races drop parts up to Epic: Common 56%, Uncommon 28%, Rare 12%, Epic 4%. Legendary parts never drop from ordinary races. You get one by winning the last level of the campaign, or with luck on an event podium.

Rule 9 · The Shop

The Shop puts three parts on the shelf every 24 hours, each from a different family. Don't like them? Watch an ad to reroll, up to three times a day. That counter is separate from your gauges, so an ad watched for fuel never costs you a reroll.

Fair play rules

  1. In-app camera only. No importing from your photo library.
  2. No photos of screens or prints. The scan is refused with a clear message.
  3. No reused photos. Each image is fingerprinted.
  4. One scan per car, per place, per 24 hours. Same model, within about 50 m, very similar photo. You can still take the picture; it just won't create a new card.
  5. Soft signals like time, motion sensors and scans per hour help spot automation.
  6. Graduated penalties: a warning, then a scanning pause, then removal from the leaderboard.

Questions

FAQ

When can I play?

StreetCaught is in active development and runs on test devices today. It will launch on iPhone first, in English, with French and Spanish to follow.

Can I scan a photo I found online or took earlier?

No. Cards only come from the in-app camera, live. The whole point of a card is that you saw that car yourself.

Why was my scan refused?

Usually one of four reasons: the photo looks like a screen or a print, the car isn't in the catalog yet, the AI wasn't sure enough (try another angle), or you already scanned that car in the same spot in the last 24 hours. Once the AI has answered, the scan uses a charge, even on a refusal. If the photo never got to it, nothing is charged.

The AI picked the wrong car. What now?

Tap "Not this car". You can pick another generation of the same model, keep the card anyway, or try another photo. When the AI can't tell generations apart, it asks you to choose from up to five instead of guessing.

Does a holo or gold card race faster?

No. Finishes are purely cosmetic. Races depend on the car's stats, its upgrades, the event and the surface.

How do you know how many cars were built?

259 entries are checked by hand against real, worldwide production figures for each generation. For the rest, the figure is estimated and the card shows an EST. badge. Estimated cars, and cars known only by their US sales, never go above Rare.

What happens if I delete a card?

The card is gone, but the car stays marked as seen in your Dex forever. There's no trash to restore it from. The garage holds 100 cars, so deleting is how you make room.

How do daily quests work?

Three a day, one easy, one medium, one hard, reset at midnight UTC. Each shows its reward up front: coins or a random part, up to Epic. Stuck on one? Watch an ad for three new quests, up to three times a day.

Do parts really make a car faster?

Yes. Parts change the car's real physics (power, weight, downforce, braking, tire grip), and the race simulation uses those numbers directly. A fully tuned car is roughly 11% faster for a Common and 20% for a Legendary. The right tires for the surface matter as much as raw power.

How do I get a Legendary part?

A guaranteed one for winning level 10 of the United Kingdom, the last level of the campaign, with three stars. An event podium can also roll one. Ordinary races and the Shop stop at Epic.

What's the point of duplicates?

Duplicates of the same model, any generation, are merged in the Workshop to upgrade that car: 1, then 2, then 3 duplicates plus coins per level. Each new copy is also another roll at a rare finish.

Why can't I put my five best cars in one hand?

Every car has a Power value and your hand has a Power budget tied to your level: 250 at level 1, +5 per level, up to 450 at level 41. Five top cars won't fit. Spend the budget where the event needs it most.

Can I race my friends?

Yes. In Friends, set the rules and your hand, and send the code the game gives you. Your friend pastes it and races it on their phone. No account needed. Send it as a file instead and your photos travel with the cards.

Does the app share my location?

No. Location is optional and only marks roughly where you caught a car on your own capture map. It stays on your phone. See the privacy policy.

How do I contact you?

Write to contact@streetcaught.com, or see the support page for quick fixes. Bug reports, a car missing from the catalog, a wrong production figure, or just an idea: everything lands in the same inbox.

Is it free?

Yes, free to download and play. Optional rewarded ads refill your gauges, and coin packs will be available to buy.
